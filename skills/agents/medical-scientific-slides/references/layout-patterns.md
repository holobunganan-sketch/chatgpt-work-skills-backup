# Layout Patterns

Use these patterns as defaults. Choose the simplest pattern that clearly supports the conclusion.

## Core Rule

Each slide should communicate one conclusion supported by 2 to 4 information modules. If more modules are needed, split the page.

## Recommended Patterns

### Left-right dual column

Use for:

- claim plus evidence
- narrative plus chart
- mechanism summary plus interpretation

Good fit when one side needs explanation and the other side needs proof.

### Three-card comparison

Use for:

- parallel drivers
- three-part frameworks
- audience segmentation
- key opportunity / risk / action views

Keep each card structurally parallel.

### Top-bottom split

Use for:

- headline finding above and supporting detail below
- executive summary plus evidence notes
- overall conclusion plus implementation items

### Center graphic with side explanations

Use for:

- pathways
- mechanism diagrams
- stakeholder relationships
- patient journey or treatment process

Prefer SVG for the center graphic.

### Chart plus takeaway

Use for:

- result interpretation
- market comparison
- trend communication
- study outcome summaries

The chart title should express the conclusion, not just name the metric.

### Timeline with key nodes

Use for:

- study milestones
- product development stages
- policy evolution
- launch sequencing

Keep each node concise. Do not overload the timeline with paragraph text.

### Comparison matrix

Use for:

- option comparison
- product positioning
- evidence comparison
- segment-by-segment strategy

Limit rows and columns to preserve readability.

### Flow or pathway map

Use for:

- diagnostic pathway
- clinical workflow
- evidence-generation process
- strategic action path

Use SVG when the path branches or loops.

### Evidence summary

Use for:

- medical affairs updates
- literature digestion
- discussion guides
- clinical communication slides

Pair short conclusions with source notes.

## Pattern Selection Heuristics

Choose by relationship type:

- parallel ideas: three-card comparison or left-right dual column
- process or causality: flow or pathway map
- temporal sequence: timeline
- quantitative proof: chart plus takeaway
- concept plus explanation: center graphic with side explanations
- strategy options: comparison matrix

When uncertain, prefer:

1. left-right dual column
2. chart plus takeaway
3. top-bottom split

These patterns are the most stable for formal presentation use.
