# Discussion and Evidence Workflow

## Source-pack authority

Use the source pack to lock the research question, study role, validated results, claim boundaries, literature map, and comparison plan. Do not introduce a new study objective or result during Discussion drafting.

Build a claim-to-source matrix with these fields:

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Discussion claim | Exact proposition to support |
| Study anchor | The present finding relevant to the claim |
| Comparison source | Paper, review, guideline, or benchmark |
| Evidence relation | Agreement, difference, extension, or uncertainty |
| Design context | Population, outcome, measurement, platform, validation level |
| Boundary | What the comparison cannot establish |

## First paragraph

Write a concise synthesis rather than a mini-Results section. Include:

1. What the study accomplished.
2. The principal finding.
3. The most important supporting pattern.
4. The finding that limits interpretation.
5. The level of claim currently supported.

Use few or no detailed numbers unless one value is essential to orient the reader.

## Comparison paragraphs

Use this internal sequence:

1. State the relevant field evidence or benchmark.
2. Identify how the present study agrees or differs.
3. Compare population, outcome, design, measurement, platform, and validation role.
4. Explain the meaningful implication for the field.
5. Close with an evidence boundary.

Prefer comparison sources in this order:

- established clinical or methodological benchmarks;
- studies with the same target population and outcome;
- studies using the same input modality or model class;
- independent evaluation and transportability studies;
- systematic reviews and reporting or bias guidance;
- implementation and clinical-impact studies.

## Recommended logical arc

Adapt the number of paragraphs to the study, but consider:

1. Overall finding synthesis.
2. Comparison with the strongest baseline or standard of care.
3. Comparison with same-domain models or interventions.
4. Transportability across populations, sites, platforms, or time.
5. Calibration, utility, implementation, or practical significance.
6. Biological or technical interpretation when supported.
7. Applicability, diversity, subgroup performance, and fairness.
8. Implications for interpretation or practice.
9. Final paragraph integrating exactly three limitations with three corresponding future-study improvements.

## Final paragraph: three limitation-improvement pairs

End Discussion with one paragraph organized around exactly three non-overlapping, high-priority limitations. Select limitations that materially affect the credibility or use of the study, rather than listing every imperfection.

For each point, include three elements:

1. Name the limitation precisely.
2. State its consequence for bias, uncertainty, generalizability, interpretation, or implementation.
3. Specify a future-study action that directly addresses that consequence.

Prefer three complementary domains when applicable:

- design, population, or data representativeness;
- measurement, modeling, or analytical robustness;
- independent validation, clinical impact, or implementation.

Use clear transitions such as "First," "Second," and "Third" under the default profile. Keep all three pairs in the same final paragraph and connect each improvement to its limitation. Replace vague phrases such as "larger studies are needed" with an actionable design, for example prospective multicenter recruitment, independent temporal validation, standardized measurement, prespecified missing-data handling, external calibration, subgroup evaluation, or impact assessment.

Do not introduce a fourth limitation, a new result, or an unsupported solution in the closing paragraph. Do not separate limitations from future work into unrelated paragraphs.

## Prohibited patterns

- Repeating Results in the same order.
- Explaining every table row or figure panel.
- Performing new statistical inference in prose.
- Claiming a mechanism without direct evidence.
- Ranking incompatible studies by one metric.
- Treating statistical significance as clinical importance.
- Ignoring contrary or weaker findings.
- Calling selection-aware assessment independent validation.
- Converting an exploratory association into a causal or deployable claim.

## Literature integrity

Use only verified references. Keep citations next to the exact claim they support. Preserve evidence level and publication status, including preprints. When evidence is indirect, label it as indirect. When no direct evidence exists, state the gap instead of filling it with analogy.
