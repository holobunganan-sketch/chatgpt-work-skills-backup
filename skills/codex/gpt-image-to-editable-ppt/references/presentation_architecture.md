# Presentation architecture

Every substantive slide requires:

1. one key message that can be stated in one sentence;
2. one primary logical relationship;
3. a visual form selected because it expresses that relationship;
4. supporting details limited to what the audience needs to understand the key message.

## Logic-to-layout mapping

- `single_conclusion`: dominant conclusion plus two to four supports.
- `comparison`: aligned columns, scorecard, paired cards, or common-axis chart.
- `process`: directional stages with explicit transitions.
- `timeline`: chronological axis with milestones and intervals.
- `hierarchy`: layered, tree, nested, or pyramid structure.
- `cause_effect`: causes converge on effects, or a causal chain.
- `problem_solution`: problem evidence, response, and expected result.
- `matrix`: two dimensions with clearly labeled quadrants or cells.
- `cycle`: closed loop with a meaningful return path.
- `evidence_chain`: claim, evidence, interpretation, implication.
- `before_after`: matched states with a visible change mechanism.
- `spatial_structure`: parts arranged according to physical or conceptual position.

## Content rules

- Prefer short labels attached to objects over detached bullet lists.
- Keep paragraphs out of the canvas unless the user explicitly needs a quotation.
- Split a slide when the minimum readable font would fall below 14 pt.
- Use color to encode categories or emphasis, not to decorate every object.
- Use repetition, alignment, proximity, and scale to establish hierarchy.
